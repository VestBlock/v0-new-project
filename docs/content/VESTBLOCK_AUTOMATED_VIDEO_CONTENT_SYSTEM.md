# VestBlock Automated Video Content System

Status: operator strategy and production contract

Created: 2026-08-20
Authority: `docs/VESTBLOCK_IMMUTABLE_BRAND_DECISIONS.md` and `docs/content/VESTBLOCK_CONTENT_COMMAND_CENTER.md`

## Outcome

Build a governed video factory that turns real buyer questions, VestBlock product capabilities, and performance evidence into a steady stream of useful content.

The system should:

- explain how people and businesses can use VestBlock;
- create searchable education and shareable scenarios;
- produce presenter, drawn, screen-demo, cinematic, and hyper-realistic formats;
- repurpose one approved idea across YouTube, Shorts, LinkedIn, Facebook, and X;
- measure what viewers actually choose and watch;
- improve future briefs and prompts from approved evidence;
- keep facts, financial claims, rights, and publishing under human control.

This system does not create a second Command Center. Supabase and the existing VestBlock Command Center remain authoritative. Obsidian remains a guarded, derived strategy surface.

## One editorial idea

Every VestBlock video should help a viewer answer:

> What is the clearest next move from where I am now?

That question unifies Capital, Deals, Opportunity, DealVault, and the Brain without narrowing VestBlock into a real-estate company, lender marketplace, credit-repair company, agency, or single software product.

## Content architecture

### 1. Capital

Help people and businesses understand readiness before they apply or commit.

Topics:

- business funding readiness;
- personal and business credit education;
- business setup and document preparation;
- grants and eligibility;
- real-estate funding preparation;
- lender fit and responsible use of capital;
- Spanish-language funding preparation.

Safe promise: understand the file, prepare what matters, and review realistic options. Never promise approval, limits, rates, deletions, score increases, awards, or timelines.

### 2. Deals

Show how better context and clearer criteria improve the next conversation.

Topics:

- property-owner paths;
- investor buy boxes;
- deal intake and analysis;
- lender criteria;
- contractor and developer opportunities;
- distressed-property scenarios;
- partner handoffs and milestones.

Safe promise: organize context and route a serious opportunity for review. Never promise an offer, match, closing, return, or financing.

### 3. Opportunity

Help businesses become easier to find, contact, and trust.

Topics:

- search and AI-search visibility;
- local business pages;
- website conversion;
- AI receptionist and lead capture;
- response-time leaks;
- practical business automation;
- partner and service opportunities.

Safe promise: improve clarity, response, and readiness. Never guarantee rankings, citations, appointments, sales, or revenue.

### 4. DealVault

Explain how cleaner records improve accountability when agreements, payouts, referrals, approvals, or milestones matter.

Topics:

- who agreed to what;
- referral and payout visibility;
- milestone history;
- document proof without exposing private files;
- proof certificates;
- team and partner accountability.

Safe promise: support clearer records and review. DealVault is not an attorney, escrow company, title company, custodian, or replacement for required written agreements.

### 5. Next Move stories

Connect the whole platform through realistic decisions instead of feature lists.

Topics:

- one person comparing several plausible paths;
- a business owner finding the quiet revenue leak;
- a deal team moving from scattered context to one clear record;
- a founder preparing for capital before applying;
- the Brain finding the next bottleneck while a human approves the action.

Spanish content is a cross-cutting expression of these pillars, not a detached sub-brand.

## Format portfolio

Do not make the channel look like one endlessly repeated AI template. The first 30-day pilot should deliberately test five production families.

### A. Pain-to-solution scenes

- Length: 20–45 seconds.
- Orientation: 9:16 first; 1:1 and 16:9 adaptations after approval.
- Structure: visible problem in the first two seconds, cost of delay, VestBlock next move, restrained CTA.
- Visuals: hyper-realistic environments, two or three people maximum, readable phone or desktop insert, no fake testimonial framing.
- Best topics: missed calls, scattered agreements, rushed applications, mismatched deals.

### B. Drawn explanations

- Length: 30–90 seconds for Shorts; 3–6 minutes for YouTube.
- Structure: one question, one diagram, one example, one takeaway.
- Visuals: HeyGen Blueprint, Marker & Sharpie, Chalkboard, Journal, Post-Its, or custom graphite/ivory/lime motion graphics.
- Best topics: capital readiness, credit factors, buy boxes, DealVault event trails, search visibility.

### C. Presenter-led guidance

- Length: 30–75 seconds or 3–8 minutes.
- Structure: direct hook, plain-language explanation, example, next step.
- Presenter mix: founder digital twin, a disclosed synthetic VestBlock guide, or voice-over only.
- Best topics: myths, checklists, comparisons, FAQs, weekly next-move briefing.

### D. Product and screen demonstrations

- Length: 60 seconds to 5 minutes.
- Structure: problem, exact workflow, what the user controls, limitations, CTA.
- Visuals: authentic VestBlock screens, cursor movement, cropped details, captions, optional presenter picture-in-picture.
- Best topics: credit review, dispute drafts, DealVault proof record, funding preparation, AI receptionist intake.

### E. Editorial and cinematic stories

- Length: 45–120 seconds or 6–12 minutes.
- Structure: one person, one consequential decision, one grounded resolution.
- Visuals: material-led documents, graphite rooms, ivory paper, restrained electric lime, close-up hands, practical environments, editorial typography.
- Best topics: master brand, Latino founder stories, property decisions, partnership accountability, behind-the-scenes system learning.

### F. Proof and case content

- Length: 30–90 seconds or 4–8 minutes.
- Use only real, permissioned evidence.
- Structure: context, baseline, change, verified result, limitation, lesson.
- Never fabricate a customer, quote, revenue result, approval, ranking, or timeline.

## Recommended production mix

- 35% searchable education and tutorials;
- 25% pain-to-solution scenarios;
- 15% product and screen demonstrations;
- 15% proof, case, and founder stories;
- 10% master-brand and behind-the-scenes content.

At least one in four published pieces should be understandable without a visible presenter. This prevents the channel from becoming dependent on one avatar or one talking-head template.

## Channel and cadence

### Thirty-day pilot

- Three Shorts per week: one scenario, one educational, one myth/checklist.
- One 3–6 minute YouTube explainer per week.
- One Spanish adaptation per week, selected from an already approved English concept and rewritten natively.
- One authentic screen demo or proof update every two weeks.
- All uploads begin as private or unlisted drafts.

Expected output: 16 original videos plus platform-native caption and crop variants. Do not count automatic crops as new creative pieces.

### After the pilot

Scale only the combinations that show both attention and business relevance. A high-view video with no qualified action is not automatically a winner. A lower-view product tutorial that creates serious requests may be more valuable.

## The governed automation loop

```text
Buyer question or verified signal
  -> scored content brief
  -> source and claim packet
  -> script
  -> compliance and brand review
  -> storyboard and production plan
  -> render
  -> technical, factual, rights, and disclosure QA
  -> human approval
  -> private upload and metadata review
  -> publish
  -> 24h / 7d / 28d measurement
  -> evidence-backed learning proposal
  -> human approval of the learning
  -> next brief and prompt version
```

### What may automate

- collecting approved public research and customer-question themes;
- scoring ideas against pillar, intent, freshness, proof, and production cost;
- creating briefs, scripts, storyboards, title options, descriptions, and crop plans;
- rendering drafts;
- running file, duration, aspect-ratio, caption, loudness, and link checks;
- uploading private drafts;
- collecting analytics snapshots;
- proposing low-risk prompt and scheduling changes;
- repurposing an approved source asset.

### What requires review

- funding, credit, grant, legal, tax, investment, or real-estate claims;
- customer stories, testimonials, and results;
- realistic synthetic scenes and their disclosure selection;
- voice or face identity;
- title and thumbnail selection for important long-form videos;
- all public publishing during the pilot;
- changes to Capital, Deals, Opportunity, DealVault, the master tagline, or approved brand direction;
- any learning that changes public claims, offers, CTAs, or audience targeting.

## Self-improvement rules

### Compare like with like

- Compare Shorts with Shorts and long-form with long-form.
- Compare the same language, pillar, traffic source, and age window when possible.
- Treat the first 24 hours, first 7 days, and first 28 days as different evidence windows.
- Normalize business actions per 1,000 views or impressions.
- Never use simulated analytics as evidence.

### Learn from the full chain

- Packaging: impressions, click-through rate, and native YouTube title/thumbnail experiments.
- Hook: first-seconds retention, Shorts stayed-to-watch, and early drop-off.
- Delivery: average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- Usefulness: saves, shares, comments, and repeated viewer questions.
- Business value: CTA clicks, qualified requests, booked conversations, and service fit.
- Efficiency: production time, render cost, revision count, and failure rate.

### Evidence thresholds

- Two comparable observations may create a low-confidence idea.
- Five comparable published assets or a native concurrent experiment are required before a pattern may change a default.
- A pattern must repeat in at least two measurement windows before it may influence cadence or format allocation.
- Financial and public-claim changes never auto-apply, regardless of sample size.

### Where learning belongs

- `experiment_results`: the hypothesis, variants, result, and confidence.
- `improvement_insights`: evidence-backed observations and recommended action.
- `prompt_versions`: approved hook, script, visual, and QA prompt changes.
- `strategy_updates`: review-required changes to pillar allocation, cadence, and CTA strategy.
- `content_assets`: the canonical brief, production lineage, and publication state.
- Command Center: operator review, approval, exceptions, cost, and next bottleneck.
- Obsidian: guarded founder decisions and derived strategy memory after the vault is healthy.

## HeyGen operating decision

HeyGen is the default presenter and hybrid renderer because it already supports:

- persistent avatar and voice identities;
- portrait and landscape Video Agent sessions;
- stock media, AI-generated visuals, and motion graphics;
- curated cinematic, handmade, and print styles;
- video-agent session iteration;
- a per-video self-evaluation log.

Current readiness on 2026-08-20:

- the HeyGen app connection is working;
- the account is on the free plan;
- there are no private avatar groups yet;
- the local HeyGen CLI is not installed.

Before the first presenter-led batch, choose one identity path:

1. founder digital twin for maximum trust;
2. named synthetic VestBlock guide for repeatable scale;
3. stock presenter for a low-commitment pilot;
4. voice-over only for drawn and screen-led content.

The recommended starting mix is voice-over/drawn for two pilots and a named VestBlock guide or founder identity for two pilots. Avatar appearance and voice require explicit approval before creation.

## YouTube Automation Agent assessment

Repository: [darkzOGx/youtube-automation-agent](https://github.com/darkzOGx/youtube-automation-agent)

Reviewed: 2026-08-20

Version reviewed: 2.6.0
License: MIT

### Useful patterns to adopt

- a production-readiness gate before autonomous work;
- persistent background jobs and visible failure states;
- factual, rights, and human approval before scheduling;
- private-by-default publishing;
- real-versus-simulated asset separation;
- 24-hour and 7-day performance snapshots;
- channel-relative baselines instead of universal view targets;
- approved learnings only;
- title and thumbnail variants selected before publishing;
- simulated analytics excluded from recommendations;
- sequential generation with a low concurrency limit to control cost.

### Why the runtime should not be installed into VestBlock now

- It creates a second Express dashboard, scheduler, SQLite database, job system, and learning store next to the existing Next.js, Supabase, cron, Command Center, `content_assets`, and improvement tables.
- Its production path is optimized for generic narration, generated images, slideshow assembly, and optional video generation, not VestBlock's premium Material Ledger direction or HeyGen identity system.
- Version 2.6.0 and the closed-loop learning system were released on the review date, so the most important features are too new to treat as production-proven.
- Open issues include narration capability reporting that still produced a silent video and a failed YouTube publish caused by invalid generated keywords. The latest release claims readiness and metadata fixes, but VestBlock should verify those claims in an isolated pilot rather than trust them blindly.
- Auto-publishing financial, credit, grant, property, or customer-result content would conflict with VestBlock's review requirements.

### Decision

Do not install or embed the runtime. Treat it as a governed pattern source.

If VestBlock later needs a proof-of-concept, run a shallow isolated clone with synthetic topics, no production credentials, no public publishing, and no customer data. The exit criterion is not “it rendered a video.” The exit criterion is whether one bounded component is clearly better than VestBlock's existing stack.

The highest-value components to adapt into VestBlock are readiness probes, measurement windows, approved learning records, simulated-data exclusion, packaging experiments, and review-state visibility.

## Definition of success

The system is working when it can reliably answer:

1. Which audience problem are we solving?
2. Which VestBlock next move does the video support?
3. What evidence and compliance limits govern the script?
4. Which format and hook are being tested?
5. Did the viewer choose, watch, understand, and act?
6. What approved learning changed the next brief?

The goal is not maximum video volume. The goal is a compounding library of clear, useful, proof-aware content that helps the right person find a better next move.
